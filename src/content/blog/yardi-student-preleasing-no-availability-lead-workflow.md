[Yardi Matrix reported on September 24](https://www.yardi.com/news/press-releases/student-housing-preleasing-tops-previous-years-in-new-yardi-matrix-report/) that student-housing preleasing across the Yardi 200 reached 93% in August 2026. That was 80 basis points above Yardi's final August 2025 estimate and 120 basis points above August 2024. [Multifamily Executive's report](https://www.multifamilyexecutive.com/rent-trends/preleasing-remains-strong-student-housing-rent-growth-moderates) also noted that 28 of those markets were fully preleased while 52 remained below 90%.

The numbers are a student-housing market signal, not a universal apartment benchmark. Yardi said the August estimate was subject to revision, and the spread between markets matters more than a national headline for any individual property. EMC2Ops is not integrated with or endorsed by Yardi or Multifamily Executive.

The operating lesson is still sharp: when inventory gets tight, “no availability” cannot be the end of the leasing workflow. A renter who cannot get the exact unit today may fit another floor plan, another property, a later move date, or a real waitlist. If the front desk only sends an apology, the portfolio loses the demand signal and the renter loses a useful next step.

For property managers managing 50+ doors, this belongs inside [apartment lead tracking automation](/use-cases/apartment-lead-tracking/). The record should show what the renter wanted, what inventory was checked, what alternative they accepted or declined, who owns the next action, and when follow-up is allowed.

## What the 93% headline does not mean

It does not mean every student-housing market is effectively full. The same reporting showed meaningful variation by school, property class, and distance from campus. It also does not mean an operator should manufacture urgency, promise a waitlist position, or push a renter toward whatever unit happens to remain.

The report is not a forecast for conventional multifamily, and it is not evidence that one property's leasing process works. High preleasing can hide weak records just as easily as slow leasing can. A team may answer quickly while recording every unavailable inquiry as “not interested,” erasing demand for a floor plan that repeatedly runs out first.

The better response is a narrow [lead-to-lease workflow](/use-cases/lead-to-lease-automation/) that treats unavailable inventory as a decision point. The workflow offers verified options, preserves the renter's choice, and stops when policy or judgment belongs with staff.

## Fix the no-availability decision first

Trigger the workflow only after the requested unit, floor plan, property, or lease term has been resolved and the approved inventory source says it cannot meet the renter's timing. Store the lookup time and result. Do not convert a stale listing, tentative hold, delayed make-ready, or uncertain future notice into a confident answer.

Then collect the smallest set of facts needed for a useful next route:

1. The property, unit or floor plan originally requested.
2. The renter's move window and any timing flexibility they volunteered.
3. The bedroom, price, location, accessibility, pet, and lease-term preferences the operator is authorized to record.
4. The source, channel, verified contact fields, and current contact permissions.
5. The inventory result and approved alternatives available at that moment.
6. The renter's explicit choice, assigned owner, next action, and due time.

Those fields turn a dead-end reply into accountable work. The [vacant-unit inquiry routing workflow](/blog/property-management-vacant-unit-inquiry-routing-workflow/) covers the availability check and first owner. This workflow starts when the answer is no and decides what should happen next.

## Use five explicit outcomes

Every no-availability inquiry should reach one of five visible states.

**Verified alternate:** Offer an available unit, floor plan, property, or lease term only when the approved source supports it. Preserve the original preference and record why the alternate fits. If the renter accepts, move the same lead record into tour or application follow-up rather than creating another guest card.

**Waitlist:** Explain the operator's approved waitlist process, confirm the renter wants that path, and store current preferences, entry time, contact permission, owner, and recheck rule. The [waitlist follow-up workflow](/blog/buildium-waitlist-follow-up-workflow/) should activate from verified returned inventory, not staff memory.

**Future-term follow-up:** When the move window is flexible but later inventory is not yet confirmable, record a dated review task. Do not represent a future notice, projected make-ready, or expected availability as reserved inventory.

**Staffed callback:** Route disputed inventory, unclear fit, policy questions, pricing exceptions, accommodation requests, or other sensitive cases to a trained person with the evidence and a response deadline.

**Clean close:** If the renter declines alternatives and does not want future contact, record a specific reason and suppress further outreach. A truthful [apartment lead lost-reason workflow](/blog/apartment-lead-lost-reason-workflow/) is more useful than hiding the case inside “not interested.”

## Automate coordination, not priority or eligibility

Automation can capture the original inquiry, normalize contact details, check an approved inventory signal, present manager-approved alternatives, record the renter's choice, create the owner task, stop obsolete tour prompts, and confirm the CRM or PMS writeback. It can also reopen the case when a verified inventory event matches the stored preference.

It should not decide who deserves scarce inventory, infer a protected characteristic, rank renters using names or neighborhoods, answer fair-housing or accommodation questions, interpret a lease, alter pricing, waive criteria, or make screening and approval decisions. Those are human-led paths under the operator's approved policies.

The [availability-sync workflow](/blog/buildium-availability-sync-workflow/) is important here because every active message should use current inventory. The [contact-preference workflow](/blog/leasing-lead-contact-preference-workflow/) matters too: agreement to receive one reply is not unlimited permission for every future campaign.

## Measure the demand the property could not serve

Track no-availability inquiries with a specific disposition, qualified renters routed to a verified alternate inside SLA, waitlist entries with current preferences and permission, future-term tasks completed when promised, and obsolete messages suppressed after the renter chooses another path.

Also report unmet demand by property, floor plan, move month, price band, and original source. Do not use that report to make individual eligibility decisions. Use it to understand where the operation is losing fit, where availability data may be stale, and whether marketing is generating demand for inventory the portfolio cannot offer.

Review writeback failures and reopened cases. If an alternate tour was booked but the original generic nurture kept running, the workflow is not complete. If a waitlisted renter must repeat every preference when inventory returns, the record did not do its job.

## Roll out with one property and one floor plan

Start where unavailable inquiries occur often enough to reveal exceptions. Define the trusted inventory source, five permitted outcomes, required fields, staff-review triggers, owner, response clock, suppression rules, and CRM or PMS destination. Run the first cases in review mode.

Test a true sellout, a unit on hold, a delayed make-ready, a renter open to another property, a renter who wants only one floor plan, an opt-out, a disputed listing, and an accommodation-related question. Expand only when the answer, next path, ownership, and record stay aligned.

Yardi's 93% preleasing figure will change with the next report. The operating standard should not: “no availability” is information, not a completed workflow.
