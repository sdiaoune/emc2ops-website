[Union launched Union Views on November 12](https://www.gounion.com/blog/why-we-built-union-views), saying multifamily leasing teams can describe the screen they want in plain language through Claude or Union's Juno assistant. The company says Views reads from the same live data as its CRM, with 41 sample datasets available today.

That is a timely answer to a familiar operating problem: today's tours live on a sticky note, aging leads sit in an export, and callbacks compete with whatever the CRM puts on screen first. But the launch also makes a critical distinction visible. **A team can generate a better view without fixing the workflow beneath it.**

For property managers managing 50+ doors, the screen is not the system. The system is the trigger, required context, owner, deadline, permitted next action, stop rule, escalation path, and verified result behind every row.

## Why property managers should care

Leasing teams do not need another generic dashboard. They need an operating view that answers five questions without opening three tabs: Which renter needs attention? Which property and stage are current? Who owns the case? What useful action is due? What evidence will prove it finished?

That is the foundation of [apartment lead tracking](/use-cases/apartment-lead-tracking/). Source capture and a visible guest card are only the start. A useful record must preserve the renter, property, source, consent, current stage, owner, last useful contact, next action, deadline, and destination receipt.

Consider a “today's tours” view. If a rescheduled tour remains on the old date, a canceled visit still triggers reminders, or a booking lacks an owner, the interface can make bad state easier to see without making it safe to act on. A board of “hot leads” has the same problem when nobody has defined what hot means, when the label expires, or which event removes the renter from the queue.

## What the Union launch does not mean

Union says Views is in early access and that it builds the first three views with each early-access operator. Its announcement places views that update records directly on the roadmap. Property managers should not read the launch as proof that every view is generally available, can safely perform every action, or replaces the underlying control work.

Nor does this article imply that EMC2Ops integrates with or is endorsed by Union. Union's claims are the news hook. The durable lesson applies to any CRM, PMS-adjacent system, spreadsheet, inbox, or custom interface: a personalized view is only as dependable as the records and rules feeding it.

The distinction appears in Union's own [November 8 product updates](https://www.gounion.com/product-updates). The company says reply-back tasks for active applicants and residents now land on the application or lease instead of the prospect page. That is not a layout preference. It is a state and identity rule that determines whether the right team sees the right work.

## Fix the queue contract before the screen

Start with one queue, such as new leasing inquiries awaiting a useful response. Write its operating contract in plain language before asking any tool to generate the view.

Define the trigger: a new website form, ILS inquiry, missed call, text, or transferred conversation. Define the required fields: source, property, renter identity, contact permission, original request, current stage, last event, assigned owner, next action, and response deadline. Then define exclusions and stop events: duplicate record under review, opt-out, confirmed booking, application start, staff takeover, closed property, or a sensitive question that requires a trained person.

The [multichannel lead intake workflow](/blog/property-management-multichannel-lead-intake-workflow/) can normalize those different events without erasing their sources. The [CRM field discipline workflow](/blog/property-management-crm-field-discipline-workflow/) keeps “new,” “contacted,” “tour booked,” and “review required” from becoming team-specific guesses.

Priority also needs a rule. A renter awaiting a promised callback may be more urgent than a newer inquiry that already received a useful answer. Use the [lead follow-up prioritization workflow](/blog/apartment-lead-follow-up-prioritization-workflow/) to rank by original arrival, renter intent, promise, stage, deadline, and exception—not by whatever row looks newest.

## What to automate behind the view

Automate deterministic checks that make the queue trustworthy: confirm the property, preserve the source event, match or create the renter record safely, validate required fields, assign an available owner, create one due time, and open the approved next action.

Use an [apartment lead next-action workflow](/blog/apartment-lead-next-action-workflow/) so every row points to one current action rather than a pile of reminders. Automate overdue alerts and backup assignment when the primary owner does not accept the work. Apply the stop rules from [leasing follow-up automation](/services/leasing-follow-up/) when the renter replies, books, applies, opts out, changes requirements, or needs staff.

The workflow should write the owner, stage, next action, deadline, summary, and outcome into the operating record, then read the destination back. [CRM workflow automation](/blog/property-management-crm-workflow-automation/) is complete when staff can see the accepted state and record ID—not when an API request or browser action merely reports success.

## What not to automate

Do not let a generated view invent stage definitions, priority rules, or business policy. Do not let a missing field silently remove a renter from the board. Keep the raw event and open an exception that someone owns.

Fair housing questions, accommodations, eligibility, pricing and concessions, application decisions, lease interpretation, complaints, identity conflicts, emergencies, and other sensitive situations belong with trained staff. The view should package the original message, verified property and renter context, current stage, prior actions, reason for escalation, and response target. A human must accept the handoff before the workflow calls it complete.

Do not hide an aging queue by changing filters. Use a visible [lead backlog recovery workflow](/blog/apartment-lead-backlog-recovery-workflow/) that preserves the original response clock and prioritizes by intent and promised action. A cleaner screen is not a recovered renter.

## Measure the queue, not the screen

Track new inquiries visible in the correct queue with complete source and property context, assignment to one available owner inside the response target, records with one current next action and due time, and stale tasks suppressed after a verified stage change.

Also measure overdue exceptions accepted by a backup owner, CRM or PMS-adjacent receipts, staff corrections caused by bad queue data, and renters missing from every active view. Review the empty states: zero overdue leads can mean excellent execution, an overly narrow filter, or a broken trigger.

## Roll out one operating view first

Start with one property group and one queue. Test a normal inquiry, duplicate contact, uncertain property, after-hours call, booked tour, changed tour, reply before follow-up, opt-out, staff absence, accommodation request, stale stage, failed writeback, and restored destination connection.

Version the queue definition and the view. Name the change owner, approval date, affected properties, expected results, rollback condition, and review window. Expand only when the team can explain why each row appears, who owns it, what happens next, and where completion was recorded.
